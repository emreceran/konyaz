Is it time to move from Excel to ERP?

Answer 12 questions based on what actually happens in your business today. The test shows where your files fall short and where it makes sense to start. It takes two minutes.

DataInformation should be entered once and read from the same record everywhere; double entry costs both time and mistakes.

1.The same information (customer, order, product) is entered separately into more than one file or program.

DataWithout a single up-to-date record, every meeting starts with arguing over whether the numbers are right.

2.Which Excel file is the latest version is sometimes up for debate.

TrackingYou should be able to see where a job stands from the records; chasing updates by phone eats up a manager's day.

3.To answer "Where is this job at?" someone has to make a call or go out to the site.

InventoryIf stock movements aren't recorded right away, shortages and over-ordering are bound to happen.

4.You don't know what's in the warehouse without a stock count, or the records and the shelves don't match.

SalesA quote should come together in a few minutes from ready price and product data, and past quotes should sit on the customer's record.

5.Preparing a quote takes more than half a day, or old quotes can't be found.

ProfitabilityUnless materials, hours and expenses are booked to the job, profit only shows up at year end, as one lump sum.

6.You can't say in numbers which job, product or customer actually makes money.

ReportingReports should come straight out of where the data is entered; a table compiled by hand is late and often wrong.

7.Preparing the month-end report or management summary takes someone a day or two.

ProcessWork should run on a record everyone can see, not in one person's head and their own files.

8.When an employee goes on leave or quits, nobody knows how a piece of work is done.

FieldThe field team should be able to log entries from their phone on the spot; late information means late decisions.

9.Information from the field team (site, service, sales) arrives at the end of the day or the next day.

FinanceWhen invoices, collections and payments sit in the same records, the cash flow picture builds itself.

10.When receivables will be collected and how much cash you'll have in the coming weeks is a guess.

OperationsWhen orders, inventory and production are linked, shortages and delays are visible in advance.

11.Missing materials, wrong shipments or missed deadlines happen several times a month.

GrowthGrowth multiplies everything that's already hard to run in Excel; putting things in order before you grow is much easier.

12.You're planning a new branch, a new product line or a bigger team in the next year or two.

    How is the score calculated?

    Each question scores 2 for "Yes", 1 for "Partly" and 0 for "No". 0–6 points means your files still carry the business, 7–14 means they fall short in several areas, and 15 or more means the problems have spread across the whole business. The areas where you answered "Yes" are listed as the ones to look at first.

    An ERP or business management system brings all these areas into one set of records: information is entered once, everyone sees the same up-to-date numbers and reports come out on their own. But you don't have to switch everything at once. More on this in the Excel to ERP article.

    Frequently asked questions

    My score came out high. Should I buy an ERP right away?

    No. First look at which areas the problems cluster in. In most companies one or two areas hurt the most; starting the move there lowers both the risk and the cost.

    We're a small company. Is this for us?

    Headcount matters less than how many places the same information is kept and how much the work depends on particular people. A 10-person company runs into exactly the same problems.
