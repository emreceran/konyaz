How is it calculated?

  • Price for target margin = cost ÷ (1 − target margin)
  • Price found by adding to cost = cost × (1 + markup). The margin at this price is always lower than the markup added.
  • Margin after discount = 1 − cost ÷ (price × (1 − discount))
  • Extra sales needed to make up = profit per unit before discount ÷ profit per unit after discount − 1

The most common mistake in quoting is treating the target margin as a percentage added to cost. The second is leaving labour hours and overheads out of the cost. Put the two together and at year end you're looking at far less profit than you aimed for. Frequently asked questions

Aren't profit margin and markup the same thing?

No. Margin is profit as a share of the selling price; markup is profit as a share of cost. If you add 25% to a product that costs 100, you sell it for 125, but your margin is 20%. For a 25% margin the price has to be 133.3.

What should I include in the cost?

Besides materials, include labour hours, any subcontractors and transport, and the share of overheads that falls on the product or job. For labour you can use the hourly cost tool. A price calculated on incomplete costs comes out too low, however correct the formula.

Why can a 10% discount wipe out half your profit?

A discount comes off the price, but the cost stays the same. So the whole discount comes out of profit. At a 25% margin, a 10% discount cuts the profit per unit by more than a third; to keep the same total profit you have to sell a lot more.

Are the numbers I enter sent anywhere?

Not unless you send them. The calculation is done entirely in your browser and nothing is saved. Your figures reach us only if you fill in and send the “Evaluate my result” form, so that we can get back to you.
