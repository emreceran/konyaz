How is it calculated?

  • Production time (hours) = quantity ÷ (output per hour × efficiency rate) + setup time
  • Production time (working days) = production hours ÷ working hours per day, rounded up
  • The start date is the first working day after the work queued on the machine is done. The finish date is found by counting the production time in working days from the start.

Working out the due date for a single order is easy. The hard part is recalculating the sequence of dozens of orders sharing the same machine every time something changes. When a rush job comes in, the finish date of every order behind it slips, and you only see that if the whole plan is kept in one place. More on this in the production management software article.

Frequently asked questions

What should I enter as the efficiency rate?

The ratio between the machine's rated speed and what it actually turns out. Breakdowns, setups, waiting for materials and defects lower this rate. If you haven't measured it, start with 60–70%; you can find your own rate with the OEE tool.

Why does work queued on the machine matter?

If the order doesn't go on the machine today, the finish date slips by that much too. The item most often forgotten when promising a date is the work that has to be finished first. For jobs that go through several machines, go by the machine with the longest queue.

Are public holidays taken into account?

No, only weekends are. If there are public holidays in between, add those days to the "work queued on the machine" field. Our Excel template has a holiday list.

Are the numbers I enter sent anywhere?

Not unless you send it. The calculation is done entirely in your browser and nothing is saved. Your details reach us only if you fill in and send the “Evaluate my result” form, so that we can get back to you.
